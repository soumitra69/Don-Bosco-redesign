Don Bosco’s Tips to School Children

  • Do not waste time — every hour is an investment in your future.
  • Do not overeat before studying to keep your mind sharp and alert.
  • Avoid bad companions as you would avoid poisonous snakes.
  • Choose studious friends and avoid idling in daydreams.
  • Keep your mind on your books when studying and master daily lessons.
  • Above all, pray — God is the true guiding light of your life.
Founder & Patron

OUR FOUNDER

SAINT JOHN BOSCO (DON BOSCO)

“Without confidence and love, there can be no true education.”

Born in the year of Napoleon’s Waterloo, Don Bosco lived through a historically significant transition period in European history marked by the Industrial Revolution and its aftermath. Don Bosco not only arose as an answer to the problems that beset the youth of his time, but also offered to the world a new educational philosophy.

He dedicated his life wholeheartedly to the education and uplifting of the young, whom he described as “that part of human society, most exposed to danger and yet so rich in promise”. He wanted them to be prepared for life and to become good citizens. Don Bosco is today remembered all over the world for his contribution to the education of youth. He has been rightly described as the “genius who gave hope to the young”.

The Genesis of Don Bosco's Mission (1841)

Don Bosco’s educational activities began in 1841, when he met a troubled teenager called Bartholomew Garelli from the tough Valdocco suburbs of Turin. The meeting was brief: a few friendly words of encouragement and a short prayer ensued, and the following Sunday, Garelli was back with six others. It was the beginning of reaching out to the youth of all classes, faiths, colours, and abilities.

Born in 1815 in a poor peasant family in Becchi, Italy, John Bosco suffered the death of his father when he was just two years old. John, who would later become the father and friend of thousands of youth, became fatherless too early in life. With indomitable courage, God’s help, and under the loving protection of his mother Mamma Margaret, John braved the vicissitudes of life.

As Margaret could not provide for the formal schooling of her children, John worked as a farm hand, waiter, tailor, shoe maker, blacksmith, and a host of other trades. In the process, he mastered eighteen professional skills that helped him later educate boys under his care to earn their livelihood with dignity.

As a priest, he roamed the crowded streets and slums of Turin, seeking out boys who needed guidance. He opened boarding schools, day schools, and technical centers called ‘Oratories’. To perpetuate this noble mission, Don Bosco founded the Salesian Society. Today, his vision flourishes in over 133 countries, with hundreds of institutions across India fostering character, academic excellence, and national integration.

Inaugurated and blessed by Rev. Father Davis Maniparamben, sdb, Provincial of the New Delhi Province on 27th January 2022. Administered by the Salesians of Don Bosco.
